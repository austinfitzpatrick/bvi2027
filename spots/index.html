The Chart Room

Candidate anchorages & adventures for our week aboard Do More

The itinerary gets decided with Captain Adrian on Day 1, around the weather and what the group wants — so nothing here is a schedule. It's a menu, organized by anchorage: pick an island and there's usually a day's worth to do around it. Each card's tags show how busy it runs — tourist magnet, medium crowds, or less busy — and which lists it's on. Read through, pick your must / want / skip, and bring it up with the group.

✦ This research was assisted by an AI and should be verified — but it gives our discussion a good launching-off point.

Trellis Bay Full Moon Party

The full moon lands on Tuesday, April 20 — right in the middle of our week. Trellis Bay on Beef Island throws the BVI's most famous full-moon bash: Aragorn's giant steel fireball sculptures burning out in the water, Moko Jumbie stilt dancers, live calypso, and barbecue along the beach. The sculptures burn in the water right off the anchorage — so we can dinghy in for the music and barbecue, then watch the fires from our own deck with a nightcap. No taxis, no cover, thirty-second ride home. Aragorn's own studio is right on the beach (open late on party night) — copper sculptures, pottery, and local crafts; the one real souvenir stop of the trip.

Medium crowdsBars & foodEvents

Foxy's Full Moon Party

Same night, opposite flavor: Foxy's at Great Harbour on Jost Van Dyke throws its own full-moon party — bonfires on the sand, live acoustic sets, and a barefoot crowd that's more conversation than spectacle. If the group wants the moon without the scene, we anchor here instead. Either way we only get the one night — Tuesday the 20th — so this is a group vote.

Norman Island

The island behind Treasure Island, a short sail from Nanny Cay — snorkeling, a floating bar, real trails, and a ring of dive sites. The main anchorage is The Bight.

Medium crowdsSnorkeling

The Caves & The Indians

Snorkel straight into the bat caves at Treasure Point, then dinghy out to The Indians — four coral pinnacles rising from the channel, some of the best snorkeling in the BVI. Both are moorings-only stops minutes from the anchorage — and both get mobbed by day boats from mid-morning, so go early.

Norman Island on Wikipedia →

Tourist magnetBars & food

The Willy T

The infamous floating bar anchored in the Bight — a converted ship you can only reach by dinghy. Jump off the top deck, earn the sticker, dinghy home. The hurricane-sunk original Willy T now lies off Peter Island as a dive site, which says something about the establishment.

willy-t.com →

Less busyHikes

Spyglass Hill & the Trails

Norman is private but keeps its ~8 miles of trails open to visitors. From the Bight, the short option climbs to Spyglass Hill — the pirates' actual lookout over the whole Drake Channel — and the long option crosses the island to Money Bay. One for the hikers while the rest hold down the Willy T.

Less busyDives

Santa Monica Rock

A seamount a mile and a half south of Norman's tip — the outermost dive site in the BVI, named for the schooner that tore open on it. The pinnacle tops out within 20 ft of the surface but the site drops past 100, and being the frontier site makes it the best pelagic roll of the dice: amberjack, horse-eye jacks, eagle rays, nurse sharks, and a strange 25-ft sinkhole in the crown. Probably too advanced for our crew — unless Georgina says otherwise.

Peter Island & Dead Chest

One island east of Norman: a resort island, the pirate-legend islet of the actual dead man's chest, and three of the BVI's best dives within a mile of each other.

Less busyDives

The Original Willy T, Sunk

The hurricane-wrecked original Willy T barge was scuttled off Peter Island's Key Bay in 2019 as a pirate-themed underwater playground — costumed pirate figures on deck, crow's nest at 35 ft, hull at 65. Dive the original in the afternoon, drink at its replacement that evening; the $5 site donation funds swim lessons for BVI kids.

Less busyDives

Painted Walls

Four narrow canyons off Dead Chest — the actual dead man's chest of the pirate song — with walls crusted floor-to-surface in sponges and corals in every color. Nurse sharks and hawksbills nap in the passageways, and at 20–50 ft the bottom time runs long and the colors stay lit.

Dive site guide →

Less busyDives

Blonde Rock

Twin pinnacles in open water between Dead Chest and Salt, rising from 60 ft to 15 below the surface under a blonde crown of fire coral. A fish magnet — schooling jacks, barracuda, turtles, the odd reef shark — and a ship-killer since the age of sail: the Royal Navy published "marks to find the Blonde Rock" back in 1824. Minutes from Painted Walls.

Deadman's Bay

One of the Caribbean's classic palm crescents, fronting the Peter Island Resort that reopened in December 2024 after a seven-year, $100M rebuild. Calm, protected water for the toys, bookable moorings, day access to the beach — and the BVI's first true steakhouse — with the Willy T wreck and Painted Walls minutes away by dinghy.

Salt, Cooper & Ginger Islands

Three quiet neighbors on the south side of the Drake Channel — the BVI's best wrecks, one of its best anchorages, and its healthiest reef.

Medium crowdsDivesSnorkelingSights & history

Wreck of the RMS Rhone

An 1867 royal mail steamer off Salt Island — the Caribbean's most celebrated wreck dive, and we're carrying our own PADI instructor and three free dives each for certified divers. The shallow stern section works for snorkelers too.

The Rhone on Wikipedia →

Less busyHikesSights & history

Salt Island Ashore

The island the Rhone sank beside is a sight of its own: an abandoned salt-raking settlement whose ponds are still harvested on occasional salt days, and whose rent was traditionally a one-pound bag of salt a year to the British Crown. A short walk from the beach passes the empty houses and ponds to the communal grave of the Rhone's sailors — a quiet, oddly moving fifteen minutes between snorkels.

Salt Island guide →

Medium crowdsSnorkelingWater toysBars & food

Cooper Island Beach Club

A solar-powered eco-resort on Manchioneel Bay you can only reach by boat. Sea turtles graze under the moorings — kayak or SUP over them in the morning calm — the microbrewery makes its own beer, and the rum bar stocks the Caribbean's largest collection — 400-odd rums. The mooring field fills by mid-afternoon, so arrive by 2 — conveniently, happy hour starts at 3.

cooperislandbeachclub.com →

Less busyDives

Wreck Alley

Four ships scuttled off Cooper's southeast bluff, close enough to swim hull-to-hull on a single tank — the cargo boat Marie L, tugs Pat and Beata, and the Island Seal, sitting upright on sand at up to 85 ft. A huge garden-eel colony carpets the sand between them, with stingrays and eagle rays cruising through.

Wreck Alley guide →

Medium crowdsSnorkeling

Cistern Point

The rocky point two minutes' dinghy (or SUP) from the Beach Club moorings — a swim-around-the-whole-rock snorkel with serious fish volume. Nurse sharks and barracuda hold in the deeper overhangs, spiny lobster pack the crevices.

Snorkel guide →

Less busyDives

Alice's Wonderland

Ginger Island's spur-and-groove reef, named for its giant mushroom-shaped coral heads straight out of Carroll. Visibility often tops 100 ft over some of the healthiest coral in the territory — eagle rays, hawksbills, huge puffers — and the easy 35–70 ft profile means every certified diver aboard can do it.

Dive site guide →

The Dogs

Uninhabited islets halfway to Virgin Gorda — no bars, no beaches worth the name, just some of the BVI's best reefs.

Medium crowdsDivesSnorkeling

Diving The Dogs

Three headline sites: The Chimney on Great Dog, a swim-up rock shaft ending in a fish-bowl cauldron of reef fish; Bronco Billy off George Dog, reputedly a Jacques Cousteau favorite; and Coral Gardens, where three small airplanes were sunk in 2019 as an artificial reef and are already coral-crusted. Day moorings only, calm-morning territory — another one to plan with Georgina, and good snorkeling for the non-divers when it's flat.

Dive guide →

Less busyDivesSnorkeling

The Sharkplaneos

Beside Coral Gardens: three retired island-hopper airplanes reborn as shark sculptures — a bull, a hammerhead, and a nurse shark with fabricated fins and teeth — sunk in 2019 in 30–50 ft of gin-clear water, next to a coral archway woven from recycled mooring line. Novice depth, so fresh certs can dive it and snorkelers can watch from the surface on a flat day.

Virgin Gorda — The Baths & Spanish Town

The southern tip of Virgin Gorda, where the granite starts — plus the town behind it and the empty mile of sand just north.

Tourist magnetSnorkelingBeachesHikesSights & history

The Baths

Giant granite boulders, grottos, and tidal pools — the single most famous sight in the BVI, mobbed by land tours from mid-morning. Being on a boat gives us an option nobody ashore has: moor off at 8 AM and swim into the grottos before the first taxi arrives, then take the boulder-scramble trail through to Devil's Bay. Nearby Fallen Jerusalem has the same boulders and nobody on them.

The Baths on Wikipedia →

Less busySnorkelingBeaches

Spring Bay & The Crawl

The Baths' boulders, one cove north, at a fraction of the crowd — NPT moorings offshore and sea-grape shade on the sand. The Crawl is a natural boulder-ringed swimming pool, calm and glassy even when it's rolly outside. The move: do the Baths at 8 AM, and when the taxis arrive, slide one cove over.

About Spring Bay →

Less busySnorkelingWater toysBeaches

Savannah Bay

A mile of usually-deserted white sand behind a barrier reef just north of Spanish Town — glassy, shallow water made for the e-foil, sea scooters, and SUPs, plus a government-installed self-guided snorkel trail with underwater marker signs on the reef. A natural day stop between the Baths and North Sound (skip it in a rare north swell).

About Savannah Bay →

Tourist magnetBars & food

CocoMaya

Latin-Asian sharing plates served toes-in-sand between Baths-style granite boulders, swing chairs at the bar, the whole room aimed at the sunset. By common consensus one of the best tables in the BVI — and one of the hardest; it books out well ahead. Runs its own full-moon parties, too. If we trade one dinner aboard for one fine meal ashore, this could be it — worth deciding early so the crew can book it.

Less busyHikesSights & history

Copper Mine Point

Cornish miners raised the chimney and engine house on this headland in the 1830s and pulled 10,000 tons of copper out before abandoning it to the waves. The ruins now stand alone against the Atlantic — a free, 15-minute stop with a huge photo payoff that stacks onto any Baths / Spanish Town run.

Virgin Gorda — North Sound

A huge reef-ringed lagoon at the island's north end — the BVI's flat-water playground and a natural overnight base.

Medium crowdsBars & foodEvents

Saba Rock

A bar-restaurant on its own one-acre rock in the middle of the sound — dinghy dock, a daily 5 PM tarpon-feeding frenzy off the dock (be tied up by 4:55), rebuilt beautifully after Irma — with the Bitter End Yacht Club across the water and Leverick Bay around the corner.

sabarock.com →

Less busyWater toys

Flat Water for the Toys

This is where we play with Do More's garage: protected, glassy morning water for the e-foil, sea scooters, SUPs, and kayaks. Next door, Eustatia Sound is the BVI's kiteboarding mecca — we don't carry kites, but outfits in North Sound do lessons and rentals if anyone's keen. Anchor here a full evening-to-morning and everyone gets a turn on everything.

Bitter End Yacht Club →

Less busyHikes

Gorda Peak

Virgin Gorda's summit — about 50 minutes up through dry forest to a 360° platform at 1,370 ft, looking down on the whole North Sound (the photo at the top of this page is that view). Short taxi to the trailhead from Leverick Bay. The best effort-to-view ratio in the islands; one for the hikers on a North Sound morning.

BVI hiking guide →

Less busyDivesSights & history

Kodiak Queen & the Kraken

A WWII Navy fuel barge — one of only five vessels to survive Pearl Harbor — rescued from a Road Town junkyard and sunk off Virgin Gorda's Mountain Point in 2017 with an 80-ft mesh kraken wrapped around her (the Branson-backed BVI Art Reef project). Storm swell has knocked the kraken's head about, but the tentacles still grip the hull at ~55 ft, now thick with coral-restoration life.

Dive BVI on the wreck →

Medium crowdsBars & foodEvents

Leverick Bay & Michael Beans

The Caribbean's self-styled last one-man band runs a gloriously silly pirate happy hour — conch-blowing contests, singalongs, prizes — Monday to Wednesday, 5–7 PM, at Leverick's beach bar. Claim a table by 4:30. Fridays add a beach BBQ with moko jumbie stilt walkers.

leverickbay.com →

Medium crowdsBars & food

Hog Heaven

A local BBQ shack on the ridge above the sound with maybe the best restaurant view in the territory — all of North Sound, Necker, Prickly Pear, and Eustatia laid out below. Ribs, buttery conch, and plantains at local prices. Short taxi up from Leverick or Gun Creek.

Medium crowdsBars & food

Nova at Oil Nut Bay

The one glossy stop boat crews say is actually worth it: an overwater restaurant at Oil Nut Bay's Marina Village — suspended pool, overwater hammocks, currently the #1-rated table on Virgin Gorda. Reservations essential (they coordinate the dock when you book). If we trade one dinner aboard for one fine meal ashore, this could be it — worth deciding early so the crew can book it.

oilnutbay.com →

Less busyWater toysBeaches

Prickly Pear — Vixen Point

The undeveloped national-park island that forms North Sound's northern wall. Vixen Point's soft white beach has calm, shallow water — a real beach afternoon without leaving the Sound, an easy dinghy from Leverick or Saba Rock. The Sand Box beach bar only opens sporadically these days; treat the beach as the destination and an open bar as a bonus.

Anegada

The far-out one: ~2–3 hours of open-water sailing north from North Sound. If the weather is good it's worth the trip.

Medium crowdsSnorkelingWater toysBeaches

The Island Itself

A low coral atoll most charters never reach — so flat you can't see it until you're nearly there. Miles of empty sand at Loblolly and Cow Wreck, flamingos in the salt ponds, snorkeling on Horseshoe Reef, and the flattest SUP water of the trip inside the anchorage.

Anegada on Wikipedia →

Medium crowdsBars & food

Lobster on the Beach

The island's famous move: radio ahead in the morning, and by sunset there's Anegada lobster on the grill at a beach shack like the Lobster Trap at Setting Point — toes in the sand, caught that day. The day-trip ferry leaves at 5 PM, so the dinner crowd is almost entirely people who sailed in. Widely rated the best meal of a BVI week — and if the group wants it louder, the Wonky Dog two docks down adds live music and a serious cocktail list. If we trade one dinner aboard for one meal ashore, this could be it — worth deciding early so the crew can plan around it.

Flash of Beauty

The best walk-in snorkeling on Anegada — a protected pocket of Horseshoe Reef with big coral heads right off the sand, ten minutes past Loblolly and far quieter. Monica's tiny beach bar behind it has a cult following for conch fritters and chicken roti.

Less busyBeachesBars & foodSights & history

Cow Wreck & the North Shore Loop

Rent a moke and loop the north shore: Cow Wreck's blinding beach and barefoot bar (named for a wrecked ship full of cow bones), the Flamingo Pond overlook — ~200 wild flamingos — and the pens in The Settlement where critically endangered Anegada rock iguanas are raised safe from cats before release. Loblolly and Big Bamboo round out the circuit.

cowwreckbeachbvi.com →

Less busySights & history

Faulkner House Museum

The clapboard home of Theodolph Faulkner — the Anegada fisherman who led 1,500 people on Road Town in 1949 and won the BVI its own legislature — kept as a period museum in The Settlement (donation entry, roughly 10 AM–2 PM). It sits on the same taxi loop as the flamingos and iguanas — a 20-minute stop. Hours are loose — have the taxi driver confirm.

Tortola's North Side & Its Islets

The home island's back side — most charters only see it on the sail between Trellis Bay and Jost. Worth slowing down for.

Medium crowdsSnorkeling

Monkey Point, Guana Island

The BVI's famous swim-inside-a-bait-ball snorkel: seasonal clouds of millions of silversides pack the cove at Guana's southern tip, with tarpon and pelicans blitzing through — plus turtles and the odd dolphin. National Parks Trust day moorings only, so it stays pristine. Go in the morning for calm.

Monkey Point guide →

Less busyBars & food

Marina Cay

Robb White's Our Virgin Island — an eight-acre, reef-ringed islet ten minutes from Trellis Bay, a Pusser's outpost for 25 years, reopened in 2023 as Marina Cay Bar & Grill under Scrub Island's operator. Palapa bar, sunset painkillers, postcard anchorage. Open Wed–Sun.

marina-cay.com →

Less busyBeachesHikes

Long Bay, Beef Island

A 30–40 minute coastal walk from the Trellis Bay anchorage ends at a long crescent of white sand facing Guana and the Camanoes — Beef Island's main swimming beach, usually near-empty. Carry water; there's nothing there but the beach.

Medium crowdsBars & foodSights & history

Cane Garden Bay & Callwood Rum

Anchor off Tortola's postcard bay and walk to the oldest continuously operating rum distillery in the Eastern Caribbean — the Callwood family has run copper pot stills in the same 400-year-old stone building for three centuries; about $5 gets the tour and tastings. The old Quito's is back as Rhythm & Sands, still with live music on the sand.

callwoodcanerum.com →

Less busyHikes

Sage Mountain National Park

The highest point in all the Virgin Islands (1,716 ft) hides a surprise pocket of elfin rainforest — mahogany, giant ferns, a dozen looped trails, about an hour for the main loop, $3 entry. The only shade-and-green hike of the trip, with Drake Channel views through the gaps. Muddy after rain.

Less busySights & history

Fahie Hill Murals

Twenty-three painted panels along a Ridge Road retaining wall — 1950s hillside life: cutting cane, terracing crops, baking cassava bread, ghost stories by lantern — led by artist Ruben Vanterpool with panels by musician Quito Rhymer and others. Free, always open. The taxi to Sage Mountain drives right past; ask the driver to pull over for ten minutes.

Jost Van Dyke & Its Islets

The barefoot island — beach bars in front, the Bubbly Pool around the back, and postcard islets off the east end.

Tourist magnetBeachesBars & food

White Bay

The BVI's most photographed beach — a strip of blinding sand behind a reef cut, lined with a dozen barefoot bars. Anchor inside the reef, swim ashore, wander bar to bar. Around the island's back side, the Bubbly Pool — a natural wave-fed jacuzzi in the rocks — makes a good walk from Foxy's Taboo.

BVI Tourism: Jost Van Dyke →

Tourist magnetBars & food

Soggy Dollar Bar

Birthplace of the Painkiller — rum, coconut, pineapple, orange, fresh nutmeg. Named for how you pay: there's no dock, so you swim in and hand over wet bills. On White Bay. Must do.

soggydollar.com →

Medium crowdsBars & foodEvents

Foxy's

Foxy Callwood has been improvising calypso at Great Harbour since 1968, under rafters hung with decades of ships' flags, t-shirts, and business cards. The all-you-can-eat BBQ with a live band runs every Friday and Saturday. Some days the man himself still sings.

foxysbar.com →

Less busySnorkelingBeachesHikes

Sandy Spit & Sandy Cay

The screensaver islets off the east end — blobs of white sand, a handful of palms, reef all around. No dock, no ferry, no bar: anchor off, wade in, and claim your own uninhabited island for an hour. Sandy Cay (Laurance Rockefeller's old island, now a national park) even has a ~20-minute loop trail over its little summit. Bring the drone — the whole-group photo alone on a speck of sand is the trip's Christmas card.

Sandy Cay on Wikipedia →

Less busyBeachesBars & food

B-Line Beach Bar

A driftwood-simple bar on its own spit of sand on Little Jost — the anti-Soggy-Dollar, and the one charter crowds keep calling the best real beach bar left in the BVI. Cold rum drinks, weekend ribs and fish tacos, cornhole and Shut the Box on an empty beach; WhatsApp them for a dinghy pickup.

Less busyBars & food

Sidney's Peace and Love

Little Harbour's old charter-fleet institution: an honor bar where you walk behind the counter, pour your own, and write it down yourself, plus lobster and BBQ dinners in a harbour a world quieter than Great Harbour. Rebuilt after Irma (and a later fire), still run by Sidney's family. The mellow counterweight to a Foxy's evening.
