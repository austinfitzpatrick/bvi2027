The Chart Room

Candidate anchorages & adventures for our week aboard Do More

The itinerary gets decided with Captain Adrian on Day 1, around the weather and what the group wants — so nothing here is a schedule. It's a menu, organized by anchorage: pick an island and there's usually a day's worth to do around it. The tags say who you'll be sharing each spot with — boaters only, mostly boaters, or the tourist crowds. Read through, pick your must / want / skip, and bring it up with the group.

A Lucky Coincidence

Full moon · Tue Apr 20Everybody comesDinghy ride

Trellis Bay Full Moon Party

The full moon lands on Tuesday, April 20 — right in the middle of our week. Trellis Bay on Beef Island throws the BVI's most famous full-moon bash: Aragorn's giant steel fireball sculptures burning out in the water, Moko Jumbie stilt dancers, live calypso, and barbecue along the beach. We anchor in the bay and dinghy in; the party comes to the boat for free.

Full moon · Tue Apr 20Mostly boatersBonfire

Foxy's Full Moon Party

Same night, opposite flavor: Foxy's at Great Harbour on Jost Van Dyke throws its own full-moon party — bonfires on the sand, live acoustic sets, and a barefoot crowd that's more conversation than spectacle. If the group wants the moon without the scene, we anchor here instead. Either way we only get the one night — Tuesday the 20th — so this is a group vote.

Norman Island — The Bight

The classic first-night anchorage, a short sail from Nanny Cay — snorkeling, a floating bar, and real trails all in one stop.

Boaters onlySnorkel

The Caves & The Indians

The island behind Treasure Island. Snorkel straight into the bat caves at Treasure Point, then dinghy out to The Indians — four coral pinnacles rising from the channel, some of the best snorkeling in the BVI. Both are moorings-only stops minutes from the anchorage.

Boaters onlyFloating bar

The Willy T

The infamous floating bar anchored in the Bight — a converted ship you can only reach by dinghy. Jump off the top deck, earn the sticker, dinghy home. The hurricane-sunk original Willy T now lies off Peter Island as a dive site, which says something about the establishment.

Boaters onlyHike

Spyglass Hill & the Trails

Norman is private but keeps its ~8 miles of trails open to visitors. From the Bight, the short option climbs to Spyglass Hill — the pirates' actual lookout over the whole Drake Channel — and the long option crosses the island to Money Bay. One for the hikers while the rest hold down the Willy T.

Salt & Cooper Islands

Two quiet neighbors on the south side of the Drake Channel — the BVI's best wreck and its best last-night anchorage.

Boaters & dive boatsSnorkelableScuba

Wreck of the RMS Rhone

An 1867 royal mail steamer off Salt Island — the Caribbean's most celebrated wreck dive, and we're carrying our own PADI instructor and three free dives each for certified divers. The shallow stern section works for snorkelers too. Ashore, a short walk passes the old salt ponds to the graves of the Rhone's sailors. Note: no diving on the first or last day, per the contract.

Mostly boatersTurtlesRum

Cooper Island Beach Club

A solar-powered eco-resort on Manchioneel Bay you can only reach by boat. Sea turtles graze under the moorings — kayak or SUP over them in the morning calm — the microbrewery makes its own beer, and the rum bar stocks one of the Caribbean's largest collections, 280-odd bottles. The obvious venue for Eric's cigars, and a favorite last-night stop before the sail home.

The Dogs

Uninhabited islets halfway to Virgin Gorda — no bars, no beaches worth the name, just some of the BVI's best reefs. A natural lunch stop on a Baths or North Sound day.

Boaters & dive boatsScubaSnorkel

Diving The Dogs

Three headline sites: The Chimney on Great Dog, a swim-up rock shaft ending in a fish-bowl cauldron of reef fish; Bronco Billy off George Dog, reputedly a Jacques Cousteau favorite; and Coral Gardens, where three small airplanes were sunk in 2019 as an artificial reef and are already coral-crusted. Day moorings only, calm-morning territory — another one to plan with Georgina, and good snorkeling for the non-divers when it's flat.

Virgin Gorda — The Baths

The southern tip of Virgin Gorda, where the granite starts.

Tourist magnetGo at 8 AMSwim in

The Baths, Before Anyone Else

Giant granite boulders, grottos, and tidal pools — the single most famous sight in the BVI, mobbed by land tours from mid-morning. The charter move: moor off at 8 AM and swim into the grottos before the first taxi arrives, then take the boulder-scramble trail through to Devil's Bay. Nearby Fallen Jerusalem has the same boulders and nobody on them.

Virgin Gorda — North Sound

A huge reef-ringed lagoon at the island's north end — the BVI's flat-water playground and a natural overnight base.

Mostly boatersEvening anchorageSunset bar

Saba Rock

A bar-restaurant on its own one-acre rock in the middle of the sound — dinghy dock, tarpon feeding at dusk, rebuilt beautifully after Irma — with the Bitter End Yacht Club across the water and Leverick Bay around the corner.

Boaters onlyToys out

The Flat Water

This is where Do More's garage earns its keep: protected, glassy morning water for the e-foil, sea scooters, SUPs, and kayaks, with adjacent Eustatia Sound serving as the local kite-and-foil playground. Anchor here a full evening-to-morning and everyone gets a turn on everything.

HikeTaxi to trailhead

Gorda Peak

Virgin Gorda's summit — about 50 minutes up through dry forest to a 360° platform at 1,370 ft, looking down on the whole North Sound (the photo at the top of this page is that view). Short taxi to the trailhead from Leverick Bay. The best effort-to-view ratio in the islands; one for the hikers on a North Sound morning.

Anegada

The far-out one: ~2–3 hours of open-water sailing north from North Sound, and the reason our week logs ~115 miles instead of 75. Wants a calm forecast; worth the spend.

Mostly boatersFull daySnorkel

The Island Itself

A low coral atoll most charters never reach — so flat you can't see it until you're nearly there. Miles of empty sand at Loblolly and Cow Wreck, flamingos in the salt ponds, snorkeling on Horseshoe Reef, and the flattest SUP water of the trip inside the anchorage.

Boaters by dinnertimeLobsterBeach bar

Lobster on the Beach

The island's famous move: radio ahead in the morning, and by sunset there's Anegada lobster on the grill at a beach shack — toes in the sand, caught that day. The day-trip ferry leaves at 5 PM, so the dinner crowd is almost entirely people who sailed in. Widely rated the best meal of a BVI week.

Jost Van Dyke & Its Islets

The barefoot island — beach bars in front, the Bubbly Pool around the back, and postcard islets off the east end.

Busy by noonBeach dayAnchor & swim

White Bay

The BVI's most photographed beach — a strip of blinding sand behind a reef cut, lined with a dozen barefoot bars. Anchor inside the reef, swim ashore, wander bar to bar. Around the island's back side, the Bubbly Pool — a natural wave-fed jacuzzi in the rocks — makes a good walk from Foxy's Taboo.

Tourist magnetPainkiller № 1

Soggy Dollar Bar

Birthplace of the Painkiller — rum, coconut, pineapple, orange, fresh nutmeg. Named for how you pay: there's no dock, so you swim in and hand over wet bills. On White Bay, so it pairs with the beach day. Obligatory.

Boaters onlySwim ashore

Sandy Spit & Sandy Cay

The screensaver islets off the east end — a blob of white sand, a handful of palms, reef all around. No dock, no ferry, no bar: you anchor off and wade in. Bring the drone; this is what it's for.
