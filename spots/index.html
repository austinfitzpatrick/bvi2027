The Chart Room

Candidate anchorages & adventures for our week aboard Do More

The itinerary gets decided with Captain Adrian on Day 1, around the weather and what the group wants — so nothing here is a schedule. It's a menu. Read through, pick your must / want / skip, and bring it up with the group.

A Lucky Coincidence

Full moon · Tue Apr 20PartyDinghy ride

Trellis Bay Full Moon Party

The full moon lands on Tuesday, April 20 — right in the middle of our week. Trellis Bay on Beef Island throws the BVI's most famous full-moon bash: Aragorn's giant steel fireball sculptures burning out in the water, Moko Jumbie stilt dancers, live calypso, and barbecue along the beach. We anchor in the bay and dinghy in; the party comes to the boat for free. (Foxy's on Jost Van Dyke throws its own mellower full-moon party the same night — bonfires and live music — if we'd rather be anchored there.)

Only Because We Have the Boat

The stuff day-trippers and resort guests mostly can't reach — the reason to charter in the first place.

Boat-onlyFull daySnorkel

Anegada

A low coral atoll ~15 miles out that most charters never reach. The standard BVI week sails about 75 nautical miles; adding the Anegada run stretches it to ~115 — worth every one. Miles of empty sand, flamingos in the salt ponds, and snorkeling on Horseshoe Reef. The one big itinerary decision: it eats a full day and wants a calm forecast.

Boat-onlyLobsterBeach bar

Anegada Lobster on the Beach

The island's famous move: radio ahead in the morning, and by sunset there's Anegada lobster on the grill at a beach shack like Cow Wreck Beach or the settlement restaurants — toes in the sand, caught that day. Widely rated the best meal of a BVI week.

Mooring-onlySnorkelFloating bar

Norman Island — Caves, Indians & Willy T

The island behind Treasure Island. Snorkel the bat caves at Treasure Point and The Indians — four coral pinnacles you moor beside, some of the best snorkeling in the BVI. Then dinghy to the Willy T, the infamous floating bar anchored in the Bight: jump off the top deck, get the sticker.

DiveSnorkelableGeorgina's domain

Wreck of the RMS Rhone

An 1867 royal mail steamer off Salt Island — the Caribbean's most celebrated wreck dive, and we're carrying our own PADI instructor and three free dives each for certified divers. Austin, Taylor, and (by then) Eric, this is the one. The shallow stern section works for snorkelers too. Note: no diving on the first or last day, per the contract.

Boat-onlySwim ashore

Sandy Spit & Sandy Cay

The screensaver islets off Jost Van Dyke — a blob of white sand, a handful of palms, reef all around. No dock, no ferry, no bar: you anchor off and wade in. Bring the drone; this is what it's for.

Beat the crowdsSwim in

The Baths, Before Anyone Else

Virgin Gorda's giant granite boulders, grottos, and tidal pools — the single most famous sight in the BVI, mobbed by land tours from mid-morning. The charter move: moor off at 8 AM and swim into the grottos before the first taxi arrives. Nearby Fallen Jerusalem has the same boulders and nobody on them.

The Classic Stops

Reachable by others, perfected by boat — the anchorages that make the BVI the BVI.

Beach dayAnchor & swim

White Bay, Jost Van Dyke

The BVI's most photographed beach — a strip of blinding sand behind a reef cut, lined with a dozen barefoot bars. Anchor inside the reef, swim ashore, wander bar to bar. Foxy's at Great Harbour and the Bubbly Pool (a natural wave-fed jacuzzi in the rocks) are on the same island.

Beach barPainkiller № 1

Soggy Dollar Bar

Birthplace of the Painkiller — rum, coconut, pineapple, orange, fresh nutmeg. Named for how you pay: there's no dock, so you swim in and hand over wet bills. On White Bay, so it pairs with the beach day. Obligatory.

Evening anchorageSunset bar

North Sound & Saba Rock

Virgin Gorda's protected inner lagoon — flat water, ringed by Prickly Pear, Eustatia, and Moskito islands. Saba Rock is a bar-restaurant on its own one-acre rock (dinghy dock, tarpon feeding at dusk), with the rebuilt Bitter End Yacht Club across the water. A natural overnight stop, and prime e-foil water.

Mooring-only resortTurtlesRum

Cooper Island Beach Club

A solar-powered eco-resort on Manchioneel Bay you can only reach by boat. Sea turtles graze under the moorings, the microbrewery makes its own beer, and the rum bar stocks one of the Caribbean's largest collections — 280-odd bottles. The obvious venue for Eric's cigars. A favorite last-night stop before the sail home.
